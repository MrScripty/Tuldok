## Assessment

The chapter is a sound basis for Tuldok's evaluation design. No material mathematical error was found in the reviewed derivations, and the checked sources support their attributed high-impact claims. Its strongest recommendation is to connect a fixed example's exact input, target and prediction to a declared checkpoint and evaluation recipe, then combine quantitative and visual evidence. It correctly keeps optimization, physical time, generalization, native numerical qualification and end-to-end utility separate.

The project example in §10.2 accurately reflects the [pinned managed CNN report](https://github.com/MrScripty/Tuldok/blob/f7543fb78f6f1cb52e45dd315efa17a4b2fe6fa6/docs/plans/generic-neural-execution/reports/managed-cnn.md): 200 Adam updates on eight fitting cases, normalized training MSE approximately 1.600599 → 0.0584414, zero of 16 restored raw predictions qualified, and prior exposure of the test family. I checked the pinned report text, not the numerical payloads or a rerun. The negative qualification should remain visible beside the successful managed execution.

## High-impact technical checks

| Chapter claim | Review result and primary evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Pressure projection, §11.1 | Correct under its displayed constant-density matched-operator assumptions: substituting `u+ = u* − (dt/rho)G p_hat`, `A = DG`, `b = (rho/dt)D u*` yields `D u+ = (dt/rho)(b − A p_hat)`. This is checked algebra, not a claim that every native solver has this exact sign or boundary treatment. [Chorin's original paper](https://math.berkeley.edu/~chorin/chorin68.pdf) supplies classical context; [Ajuria Illarramendi et al., equations 2–3](https://doi.org/10.1017/dce.2022.2) explicitly show the matching continuum projection. |
| Alternating pressure example, §11.1 | Correct for the stated standard periodic second-difference stencil: pressure RMS is epsilon, Laplacian/residual RMS is `4 epsilon / h²`; `epsilon=0.001`, `h=0.01` gives 40. The warning about collocated central-gradient nullspaces matters. [Tompson et al., §3](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/tompson17a/tompson17a.pdf) explicitly distinguish MAC face velocities/cell scalars and central-difference nullspaces. |
| Gauge and residual, §§11.2–11.4 | Constant pressure offsets are harmless only when the actual boundary/gauge permits them; one global mean can be insufficient for disconnected domains. `p − p_hat = A⁻¹r` is correct for an invertible gauge-fixed system. The text already prevents using pressure MSE or small residual as a universal physical certificate. Native operator/gauge implementation remains required. |
| Relative and variance-scaled field errors, §6.2 | The authored 99/101 target example is correct. [The Well, appendix E.3](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/4f9a5acd91ac76569f2fe291b1f4772b-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.pdf) uses centered target variance and places epsilon inside the squared denominator before taking the square root. The chapter deliberately gives another possible convention and says to state epsilon placement; implementations must not equate these formulas or reuse the benchmark name without its definition. |
| Physical balance and rollout, §12 | The integral conservation equation follows the divergence theorem for the fixed-domain setting displayed. The uniform forced-flow solution, increasing energy, persistence error and Lipschitz rollout recurrence are correct under their stated assumptions. [PDEBench's first-party metrics](https://github.com/pdebench/PDEBench/blob/main/pdebench/models/metrics.py) confirm multiple error categories beyond global averages. No universal stability bound is claimed. |
| Calibration and predictive uncertainty, §13 | [Guo et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a.html) support classification calibration and temperature scaling, not fluid-field coverage. [CQR, theorem 1](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2019/file/5103c3584b063c431bd1268e9b5e76fb-Paper.pdf) supports finite-sample marginal coverage under exchangeability; the chapter correctly excludes automatic conditional/simultaneous/shift guarantees. [Adaptive Conformal Inference](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/hash/0d441de75945e5acbc865406fc9a2559-Abstract.html) concerns long-run online coverage frequency. [Ovadia et al.](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2019/hash/8558cb408c1d76621371888657d2eb1d-Abstract.html) support testing calibration under shift, with the classification-to-physics limitation retained. |
| Human scoring and learning, §§2,14 | Correct: a score used to select a checkpoint is model selection, not automatically a training signal. [Williams](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00992696) supports stochastic score-function learning; [Christiano et al.](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2017/hash/d5e2c0adad503c91f91df240d0cd4e49-Abstract.html) learn a goal from segment preferences and then behavior; [DPO](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/a85b405ed65c6477a4fe8302b5e06ce7-Abstract.html) supplies a language-policy/reference formulation, not a generic deterministic-field update. [Gao et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/gao23h.html) use a synthetic gold reward model; the chapter preserves that limit. |
| Hybrid solver utility and uncertainty, §15 | The serial fallback expectation and 4 ms/11 ms examples are correct; conditional fallback cost is necessary. [Ajuria Illarramendi et al., §7](https://doi.org/10.1017/dce.2022.2) explicitly distinguish inference, complete projection and full fluid iteration at matched error criteria. [Agarwal et al.](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/hash/f514cec81cb148559cf475e7426eed5e-Abstract.html) support exposing few-run uncertainty; their RL methodology is appropriately caveated rather than copied as a universal estimator. |

The visual cautions are also technically appropriate: fixed-scale comparisons preserve magnitude, velocity speed cannot recover direction, opaque views can hide interior errors, and an uncertainty layer must correspond to an actual model output. [Adebayo et al.](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2018/hash/294a8ed24b1ad22ec2e7efea049b8737-Abstract.html) support the separate warning that attractive saliency can fail model/data-dependence checks. The rainbow-map publisher link did not load in this review tool; I did not independently read that reference, so no additional claim depends on it here.

## Precision improvements before treating the chapter as a contract

These are implementation hazards and clarifications, not evidence that the synthesis is invalid:

- Name plots by axis. The chapter uses “learning curves” for epoch/update traces; scikit-learn also uses that term for score versus training-set size. Product labels should say “validation loss versus epoch,” “error versus simulated seconds” or “score versus training examples” as applicable.
- Make `not measured`, `not supported`, failed, cancelled, nonfinite and zero distinct states. Do not produce synthetic uncertainty, gradients or history for adapters that never emitted them. Test-only metrics must not appear through another plot/export route.
- The REINFORCE identity in §2.4 requires the usual interchange/support/integrability conditions and a reward without a missing direct parameter derivative. It is appropriately conditional, but an implemented reward API must state this explicitly; a trainable evaluator is not automatically an exogenous reward.
- In §13.4 use “retained fraction” for accepted examples and “risk among retained cases” for error. Reserve “coverage” with a qualifier for predictive interval/set coverage; acceptance coverage is a different denominator.
- A synchronized picture requires a declared comparison policy. Never silently recenter pressure, interpolate time, resample meshes, collocate staggered components or infer unit equivalence. Difference values should be computed from verified numeric fields, with any display downsampling labeled.
- Reference-based tolerances, normalized residuals, binning, masks and aggregation need versioned definitions. The chapter gives examples and categories, not universal thresholds. Changing a metric definition must not silently rewrite old results.

## Missing implementation contracts

The chapter supplies the right questions but leaves the following necessary designs open:

1. **Evaluation identity and admission.** Define a versioned immutable evaluation artifact binding run/material/runtime, checkpoint hash, frozen probe membership and protected-family/split identity, transform/reconstruction/inference recipe, evaluator implementation and metric definitions. For fields include shape, coordinates, orientation, centering, stage, physical time, units and mask identity. Hashes should bind retained bytes; a friendly artifact role alone must not authorize a privileged renderer or numerical evaluator.
2. **Observation versus fitting.** Define whether a point is pre-update, post-update, an epoch aggregate, fresh fixed-set evaluation, or one complete noniterative fit. Live generic progress currently exposes only the latest valid bounded event; complete history/checkpoint predictions require explicitly retained artifacts and a cadence/storage budget. A final selected checkpoint cannot reconstruct earlier checkpoint predictions.
3. **Bounded storage and rendering.** Declare maximum checkpoints, examples, arrays, scalar samples, artifact bytes and expanded sizes; paging/downsampling/cancellation behavior; durable partial/failure semantics; and what a cold reader verifies before display. Full tensors per optimizer update can overwhelm an otherwise bounded run.
4. **Split and exposure policy.** Reuse current frozen-release/protected-family and durable test-exposure owners across charts, slices, exports, comparison caches and human review. Fixed validation probes are repeatedly consulted development evidence. Moving an exposed family into a new ZIP or changing its interval does not restore independence.
5. **Statistical experiment design.** Record the estimand, independent sampling unit, paired cases, fit seeds, failed runs, aggregation weights, intervals and assumptions. Very few groups may support only descriptive evidence. Seeds, frames, voxels and review ratings cannot all be pooled as independent replications.
6. **Selection and review.** Define declared metrics, mandatory constraints, selection criterion and provenance of the chosen candidate. Store human rubric/version, exact reviewed output and display settings, rater/ordering/tie/reason metadata and edits as evidence. A later preference-learning workflow needs its own training recipe and independent evaluation.
7. **Scientific evaluator ownership.** Keep native residual, boundary and physical-acceptance computations with the producer/domain adapter that knows the solver. Generic Tuldok should verify and display typed retained evidence. It cannot invent a physical contract from arbitrary arrays.

## Recommended first delivery

Extend current verified classification artifacts with epoch history, selected-checkpoint information, row-normalized confusion, per-class support metrics and per-example probability inspection. Preserve existing clickable error counts and frozen-target-to-current-correction behavior. Add same-release comparisons before broad cross-release comparisons. These are immediately useful without claiming unsupported telemetry or retraining capabilities.

Then introduce fixed probe/checkpoint evaluation artifacts for generic training and synchronized field comparison using the contracts above. Add native operator maps, rollout/route/timing evidence where the domain producer can actually supply them. Treat calibration fitting, probabilistic field coverage, repeated-seed orchestration and preference-driven training as later explicit capabilities, each with an independent validation design.

No application code, training runs, paper text or numerical thresholds were changed by this review. Supporting external findings are in [primary-sources.md](primary-sources.md).
